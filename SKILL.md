# Venture Thinking

An idea is not a plan — it is a bundle of **assumptions** that are still untested. This skill runs the loop that turns an idea into a **hypothesis**, finds the one assumption most likely to be wrong, and designs the **cheapest test** that could kill it. The person leaves with a bet they can act on this week, not a plan they will polish for a month.

Amateurs ask *"is my idea good?"* This loop asks *"which assumption is riskiest, and what is the cheapest way to find out?"*

## Stance

Hold these while running the loop — they override the default urge to help build:

- **Idea → hypothesis.** Restate every idea as a claim that can be proven wrong. "An app for X" is not testable; "clinic owners lose 3 hours a day to manual WhatsApp orders and will pay Rp1jt/mo to stop" is.
- **Refuse the solution jump.** When someone leads with a solution ("I'll build a CRM"), pull back to the problem first. The solution is a guess about a problem; name the problem before defending the guess.
- **Hunt the riskiest assumption, not confirmation.** Your job is to find the belief that, if false, sinks the whole thing — then attack *that*, cheaply, before anything gets built.
- **Painkiller over vitamin.** A painkiller is something the customer *must* have; a vitamin is nice-to-have. Only painkillers sell easily. Grade the pain honestly.
- **Match rigor to reversibility.** A **reversible** decision (copy, pricing test, MVP scope) is cheap to undo — decide fast and test. An **irreversible** one (big hire, capital, long contract, new market) is expensive to undo — that is where deep reasoning earns its keep. Do not deep-think reversible bets; do not gamble irreversible ones.
- **Close the gap, don't just point at it.** Flagging a risk is half the job. Before handing back a "go find out" homework assignment, spend your own research budget trying to shrink it: desk research *and* social listening (Reddit, X, community groups, app-store/review-site complaints — searched by you, now) for this exact customer in their own words. Only ask the person to act on what's left — specifically, willingness-to-pay, which no search can answer (see [`references/research.md`](references/research.md)).
- **Propose, don't just object.** If a stronger wedge, cheaper channel, better price point, or an adjacent problem with better economics is visible, say so explicitly with reasoning — do not just critique their idea and stop. Skepticism without a counter-offer is a commissioner's job, not this skill's.
- **Never stop at "kill it."** The loop isn't done until you've hunted for the sharper version. If the idea survives testing, name the gap it still doesn't cover and propose the most inventive fix you can find for it — a wedge, a feature, a channel, a business-model twist — not just a pass/fail stamp. If the test has already killed it (or clearly will), don't stop at red: use the same research to pin down exactly what killed it, then propose one concrete pivot that removes that specific flaw and would plausibly survive a retest. "It's dead" is never the final artifact — "here's the version that isn't" is.
- **Ground the idea in real domain vocabulary, not invented placeholders.** Before finalizing FRAME or naming assumptions, check the idea against real industry/role/process knowledge instead of guessing generically — see [`references/domain-knowledge.md`](references/domain-knowledge.md).

## The loop

Locate where the person is, run that stage, then push to the next. Do not run stages they are past.

1. **FRAME** — Force the problem into focus before any solution. Answer, out loud, for their idea: *who exactly*, *what problem*, *why it happens*, *how often*, *how painful*, *what they use today*, *will they pay*. Vague answers here are the finding — chase them down; ground the customer/role/process in real domain knowledge rather than a generic guess ([`references/domain-knowledge.md`](references/domain-knowledge.md)). Full battery: [`references/framing.md`](references/framing.md). **Done when:** the idea is stated as one falsifiable hypothesis sentence naming the customer, the pain, and the willingness-to-pay.

2. **ASSUME** — List everything that must be true for this to work (customer has the problem / it is painful enough / they will pay $X / you can reach them profitably / you can deliver it / it can get large). Rank each by *risk × uncertainty*. Cross-check against the domain's real value chain ([`references/domain-knowledge.md`](references/domain-knowledge.md)) — an assumption that skips a layer of who-does-what-with-what is usually the unexamined one. **Done when:** the single riskiest assumption is named — the one that is both most damaging if false and least known today.

3. **TEST** — Do not open with an instruction to the person. First, run desk research *and* social listening yourself, in this turn: search Reddit, X, Facebook/community groups, App/Play Store and G2/Capterra reviews, and forums for this exact customer + problem in their own words. Unprompted complaints and "how do I deal with X" threads settle most of what a first interview round exists to find — pain is real, how it's described, how urgent it is. State plainly what that search did and didn't settle. Only what's left — specifically "will they pay this price for this offer" — needs a person-run test, and it should be the smallest one that can answer it: landing page + ad spend, pre-order/waitlist, concierge/manual MVP, pilot. Reach for open-ended interviews only if social listening found nothing usable, and even then scope it small (3–5 people, days not a week). Not a finished product. State the pass/fail signal *before* running it. **Done when:** there is one test, its cost, its turnaround time, and the observable result that would confirm or refute the assumption — and it's the smallest test left after your own research, not the first one that came to mind. Stage map (BUILD→SELL→LEARN→SCALE) if they are further along: [`references/loop.md`](references/loop.md).

4. **BET** — Convert to one concrete Horizon-1 action for this week (e.g. "launch a landing page with two pricing tiers by Wednesday" or "interview 5 clinic owners by Friday" — whichever the TEST stage actually produced), while keeping the ambitious Horizon-3 vision as the North Star. **Done when:** the person has a single next action with a deadline.

Close every session with five artifacts: **the hypothesis**, **the riskiest assumption**, **the cheapest test + its pass/fail signal**, **the one action this week**, and **the sharper version** — if the idea is still alive, the gap it doesn't cover yet plus the most inventive fix for it ("no better angle found" is only valid after genuinely looking); if the test killed it (or clearly will), the exact cause of death plus one concrete pivot that removes it. If any is missing, the loop is not done.

## Research: desk, social listening, and true primary

Use **web search / browser tools** during OBSERVE, FRAME, and TEST to run two kinds of research yourself, before assigning anything to the person: **desk research** (existing solutions, competitors, market size, pricing, trends) and **social listening** (searching Reddit, X, community groups, App/Play Store and review-site complaints for this exact customer talking about this exact pain, unprompted, in their own words). Together these settle most of what a first interview round exists to find — do them in the same turn you're asked, not as a suggestion.

Search **globally, not just the local market or local language** — run both kinds of research in English and other relevant languages against worldwide sources, not only Indonesian ones, even for an Indonesia-first idea. A sharper wedge, a cheaper channel, or a cautionary already-tried solution can come from anywhere.

What's left has a hard ceiling neither search can reach: whether this customer will actually *pay* this price for this offer. That's the only thing that needs a person-run test — and it should be the smallest one that answers just that question, not a default week of interviews. How to run each well: [`references/research.md`](references/research.md).

## When NOT to run this

- They have already validated and committed, and are now building — help build, do not re-litigate the idea.
- The decision is small and reversible — just make the call and move on; this loop is for bets worth reasoning about.
