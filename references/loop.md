# The full loop: OBSERVE → SCALE

FRAME/ASSUME/TEST/BET in SKILL.md are the working core. This is the whole arc, for locating someone further along.

```
OBSERVE → FRAME → TEST → BUILD → SELL → LEARN → SCALE
```

1. **OBSERVE** — Look at the world through a *problem* lens, not an *idea* lens. Scan for: friction ("why is this so clunky?"), waste ("why is this manual?"), delay ("why 3 days?"), repetition ("why do they do this over and over?"), expense ("why so costly?"), fragmentation ("why 5 tools?"), behavior change ("people are starting to do X differently"). Ideas are a *consequence* of noticing a problem.

2. **FRAME** — See `framing.md`. Problem before solution. Idea → hypothesis.

3. **TEST** — Cheapest possible evidence: interview, landing page, prototype, manual/concierge service, pre-order, pilot. Define the pass/fail signal first. Find the mistake fast and cheap — that is the whole point of an MVP.

4. **BUILD** — Build the *least* that delivers real value. Not the product you imagine — the smallest slice that lets a real customer get the outcome.

5. **SELL** — The honest gate. "Users like it" is not the signal; "users *pay*" is. Charge before you scale.

6. **LEARN** — What turned out to be wrong? Update the hypothesis. Good founders are wrong often — they just find out cheaper and faster than everyone else.

7. **SCALE** — Only *after* evidence: automation, marketing, hiring, capital, expansion. Scaling an unvalidated thing multiplies the mistake.

## Two patterns that sharpen the idea

- **Wedge.** Do not try to win the whole market at once. Find a tiny, specific entry point ("AI receptionist for dental clinics", not "AI for all businesses"), win product-market fit there, *then* expand outward.
- **Painkiller vs vitamin.** The bigger the pain, the easier the sale. A slick analytics dashboard is a vitamin; a system that stops Rp50jt/mo of fraud is a painkiller. Aim for painkillers.

## Thinking horizons

Keep three time layers at once — ambitious far, concrete near:

- **H1 — this week:** one concrete, realistic action ("interview 10 customers").
- **H2 — 3–12 months:** if it works, what does it become? ("100 paying customers, Rp100jt ARR").
- **H3 — 3–10 years:** the North Star, allowed to be wildly ambitious ("an AI operating system for small businesses").

Vision → hypothesis → experiment → evidence → learning → new hypothesis. Not vision → perfect plan → execute → success. The real world does not run the second way.
