# Venture thinking: the skill tree

Venture thinking is the umbrella — the ability to turn uncertainty into testable opportunity, then turn proven opportunity into a business. It decomposes into 13 sub-skills. Use this map for deliberate self-study; the SKILL.md loop already exercises most of them in practice.

```
VENTURE THINKING
│
├── 01. Opportunity Recognition   — spot opportunity in problems & change
├── 02. Problem Discovery         — find & define the real problem
├── 03. First-Principles Thinking — break a problem down to fundamentals
├── 04. Systems Thinking          — see how parts of a system connect
├── 05. Market Thinking           — size, timing, competition, wedge
├── 06. Customer Psychology       — why people buy, switch, and stay
├── 07. Business Model Thinking   — pricing, revenue, cost, margin
├── 08. Hypothesis & Experiment   — turn assumptions into cheap tests
├── 09. Strategic Decision Making — bets under uncertainty; reversible vs not
├── 10. Resource Allocation       — where scarce time/money/focus goes
├── 11. Execution                 — turn thinking into concrete action
├── 12. Learning & Iteration      — build → learn → adapt
└── 13. Scaling                   — automation, hiring, capital, expansion
```

## The one mindset underneath all of it

Entrepreneurship is not the ability to know the future. It is the ability to make good decisions *while the future is still unknown* — and to be wrong faster and cheaper than everyone else.

Train the chain, not a list of "100 business ideas":

> observe → find a problem → form a hypothesis → name the assumptions → seek evidence → make a small bet → learn → scale the bets that prove out.

That chain is what makes someone look like "wow, this person thinks so far ahead." It is not talent. It is a trainable system.
