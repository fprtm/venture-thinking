# FRAME: the interrogation battery

Reached from the FRAME stage. Every vague answer is a finding — do not accept a blur, chase the specific. The goal is one falsifiable hypothesis sentence.

## The seven questions

1. **Who — exactly?** Not "UMKM" or "businesses". *Which* one: dental clinics? online sellers? agencies? salons? The narrower, the sharper the rest of the answers get.
2. **What is the real problem?** Behind "hard to manage customers" sits a specific failure: forgets to follow up? data scattered? can't tell which lead is hot? can't broadcast? Name the actual broken thing.
3. **Why does it happen?** Root cause, not symptom. This is where a real wedge often hides.
4. **How often?** Daily / weekly / monthly. Daily pain is a business; annual pain rarely is.
5. **How painful?** A minor annoyance, or losing millions? Grade it: is this a **painkiller** (must-fix) or a **vitamin** (nice-to-have)? Only painkillers sell easily.
6. **What do they use today?** There is always a current solution — Excel, WhatsApp, Notion, a notebook, an admin person, existing software. If there is *no* current workaround, the pain may not be real.
7. **Will they pay — and how much?** The decisive one. Is the pain sharp enough that they will spend money to remove it? A "yes, I'd use that" without "yes, I'd pay $X" is not validation.

## Output of this stage

Collapse the answers into ONE sentence of the form:

> **[specific customer]** loses **[quantified pain]** because **[root cause]**, currently copes with **[current solution]**, and will pay **[price]** to remove it.

Example:
> Dental clinics lose ~3 staff-hours/day handling repetitive WhatsApp questions, currently cope with a part-time admin, and will pay Rp500k–2jt/mo to automate it.

If you cannot fill every bracket with something concrete, the missing bracket IS the next thing to go learn.
